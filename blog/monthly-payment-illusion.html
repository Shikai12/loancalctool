The Monthly Payment Illusion Banks Use to Trick You

James Whitmore
May 28, 2026 Loans
Article Middle Ad — Loading...

Here's a trick that's made banks billions: the monthly payment illusion.

I had a client — call him Rick — who was comparing two auto loans. Loan A: $28,000 at 6.9% APR, 60 months, monthly payment $553. Loan B: $28,000 at 7.9% APR, 72 months, monthly payment $492. Rick thought Loan B was cheaper because the payment was $61 lower per month.

He was wrong. Loan B would cost him around $3,400 more in total interest.

Banks know that most people focus on the monthly number. So they stretch the loan term to make the payment look smaller, even if the interest rate is higher. It's not illegal. It's just sneaky.

Let me break it down.

$28,000 at 6.9% for 60 months: total interest around $5,200. Total cost around $33,200.

$28,000 at 7.9% for 72 months: total interest around $8,600. Total cost around $36,600.

The difference is $3,400. That's a used car. Or a vacation. Or a new roof. Rick was about to sign away $3,400 because he only looked at the monthly payment.

Here's the rule: always ask for the total cost of the loan, not just the monthly payment. And ask for the APR (which includes fees). Then compare those two numbers across offers. The monthly payment is a distraction.

I see this all the time with mortgage refinancing too. A lender will offer to lower your monthly payment from $1,500 to $1,350. That's $150 less per month. Sounds great. But they might stretch your term from 15 years back to 30 years. Even with a lower rate, you could end up paying more in total interest.

I'm not saying never take a lower monthly payment. If you're struggling to make ends meet, a lower payment can save you from default. That's valuable. But if you have room in your budget, take the shorter term and pay less overall.

Rick ended up taking Loan A with a 60-month term. He paid it off in 48 months by adding $100 extra each month. Total interest paid: around $3,400. He saved around $1,800 compared to the original estimate. He called me when he made the last payment. I literally cheered.

The monthly payment illusion works because banks know you're tired, stressed, and just want the number to be lower. Don't fall for it.
